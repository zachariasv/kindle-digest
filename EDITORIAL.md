# Editorial brief

The reader is a Stockholm-based VC analyst and applied-maths master's student. Goal: be well read, never caught off guard by a current event, and have real technical depth on AI.

## Daily edition (written by 06:45 Stockholm time)

Write `editions/YYYY-MM-DD.json` (same schema as earlier editions), then run `python build.py`.

Sections, in this order:

1. **Everyone's talking about**: 3-5 one-to-two-sentence items, the stories most likely to come up at dinner or in a meeting today.
2. **Geopolitics**: wars, great-power moves, anything that shifts the global picture. Add "what to watch" where useful.
3. **US and Europe**: big political developments only.
4. **Sweden**: politics and general news, a bit deeper than the rest.
5. **Tech and AI**: company news, launches, funding, policy. End items with a "why it matters" line when non-obvious.
6. **Breakthrough of the day**: ONE technical item (new algorithm, optimizer, architecture, training method, proof) explained properly: the mechanism, why it beats prior work, key numbers. Write it for a reader with a strong maths background. Optional "Also on the radar" with 1-2 one-liners.
7. **Stockholm VC**: Breakit / Di Digital. Notable rounds, fund news, people moves. Skip the section when nothing matters.
8. **Week in review** (Sundays only): 3 threads that connect the week's stories.

Must-reads: exactly 3 original articles per day, the best pieces to read in full. Prefer outlets that are free and render cleanly as plain text (Al Jazeera, Euronews, SVT, SR, AP, BBC, Ars Technica, lab and research blogs, Quanta). Avoid paywalled sites (FT, WSJ, DN+, Breakit).

## Style

- Plain, conversational English. No jargon for its own sake.
- Write everything in your own words. Never copy sentences from sources, never paste article text.
- Every item lists its source(s) and date.
- Total length: about a 10-12 minute read on weekdays, a little longer on Sundays.
- Verify facts against at least one primary or reputable source. Flag claims that are only reported.
- Don't repeat yesterday's story unless something actually changed. If it did, say what's new.
